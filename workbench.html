Sourcing the CFRP honeycomb panels, one decision at a time

Six steps, from what we need to what I would do. Each step has a few inputs; move one and the date, cost and risk update straight away. Inputs tagged placeholder are my estimates until suppliers reply.

What we need

Each satellite needs six to eight lightweight panels, the largest about 700 by 500 mm and 10 to 25 mm thick. Each panel is a sandwich: thin carbon-fibre skins glued to an aluminium honeycomb middle, with metal inserts so equipment can be bolted on. Test sets come first, and flight panels must reach the satellite team by the date below.

Panels to buy

What the words mean
CFRP
Carbon-fibre reinforced plastic: carbon threads set in hard resin. Very stiff for its weight.
Honeycomb sandwich panel
Two thin skins glued to a honeycomb middle. Like corrugated cardboard, it is stiff because the skins are held apart.
Prepreg
Carbon cloth already soaked in resin, kept frozen until use, then cured hard in an oven.
Insert
A threaded metal bush glued into the panel so a bolt has something to grip.
Structural model
Panels built early to check fit, weight and stiffness. Not flown.
Engineering model
A trial build of the real design, used to find problems.
Qualification model
A set tested harder than flight to prove the design. Not flown.
Protoflight
Skipping the qualification model and testing the flight panels themselves, a bit more gently.
RFI and RFQ
Requests to suppliers: first for information, then for a price.
Export licence
Government permission to ship a controlled material. Aerospace carbon fibre needs one because it could also go into weapons.
Critical path
The chain of tasks with no spare time. Any delay on it delays delivery.
Coupon
A small test sample made the same way as the real part, then broken to measure strength.

Export control check

Governments control strong, stiff carbon fibre because it can also be used in missiles. The test uses stiffness and strength per unit of weight. One list (ECCN 1C010.b) catches fibres above both limits; another (1C210.a) catches fibres above either one. Every grade below lands on at least one list, so we need a licence whichever fibre we pick. Change a density to check the maths.

Backup supplier

The satellite cannot fly without these panels. Relying on one supplier with no backup is the riskiest way to buy something that critical, so pick how much cover to pay for.
